# Jordy Cutz — Growth Plan

Research date: 10 September 2026. Covers SEO, branding, PR, design, and the question of
whether search rankings can be shortcut. Every claim below was either verified directly
against the live site or sourced; where sources were thin or disagreed, that is said plainly.

Three lists: **what is already done**, **what Jordy has to do himself**, and **what is left
to build**. The middle list matters most. Almost none of the highest-impact work is website work.

## Can rankings be shortcut?

Short answer: no, and the attempt is what would actually cost him the business.

**Google has not paywalled local search, but it is charging rent.** Roughly two thirds of
Google searches now end without a click, and paid units inside the local map pack expanded
sharply through 2026. But that encroachment concentrates on informational searches. For
transactional local intent — the "barber near me" pattern that books haircuts — the map pack
still appears the overwhelming majority of the time, and AI answers rarely displace it.
Local Services Ads, the unit that outranks everything, does not appear to cover barbers in
Maryland. **The map pack is winnable here without ad spend.**

**What would end him.** Manipulated reviews now carry US federal civil penalties above
$53,000 per violation, and Google removed 292 million policy-violating reviews in a single
year. The specific trap is review gating — asking only happy clients. Google's standard
remedy is deleting every review posted during the gating period, which would take his real
forty with it. A hard suspension removes the listing from Search and Maps completely, and
reinstatement commonly runs weeks. For a business whose pipeline is Maps plus Booksy, that
is a direct income event.

**Bought clicks and bot traffic.** Click behaviour is a genuine ranking input — this was
established under oath in the US antitrust trial. But no credible controlled study shows
that purchased clicks produce durable gains, every affirmative claim traces to a vendor,
and automated querying is a named spam violation. The expected value is near zero and the
downside is unbounded.

**Safe, and underused:** asking every client for a review, replying to all of them,
completing every field on the business profile, real local press, genuine partnerships.
**Unsafe:** incentives for reviews, gating, keyword-stuffed business names, virtual
addresses, bought links, bought clicks, spun content.

The test for anything an SEO seller pitches: would you be comfortable if Google, the FTC,
and your customers all saw exactly how it works? Everything in the safe column passes.

## Already done

Committed and live.

**Search foundation**
- Structured data rebuilt as a full graph — the business with verified coordinates, nine
  service areas, all eleven services as priced offers, a booking action, and a separate FAQ
  block. The star rating was deliberately left out: marking up your own reviews on your own
  site violates Google's policy and can forfeit rich results entirely.
- Meta description cut from 223 characters, which truncated mid-sentence, to 144.
- Titles rewritten to lead with what people search for rather than the brand name.
- `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` with language annotations, and a branded 404 page. None existed.

**Spanish is now a real page**
Previously the Spanish copy existed only inside JavaScript, so search engines had no Spanish
URL to rank and roughly 140 translated strings were invisible. There is now a genuine page at
`/es/`, generated by `build.py`, with reciprocal hreflang. The toggle is real links, so both
versions are crawlable and shareable, and the URL decides the language in both directions.
This is the least contested opening he has: Anne Arundel County is around 10% Hispanic and
no Annapolis barber is optimised in Spanish.

**A corrected address**
The studio is in **21403**, not 21401. Booksy has it wrong and the site inherited the error.
Confirmed against the USPS geocoder. Inconsistent addresses split local ranking authority.

**Accessibility**
- A focus system. There were previously zero outline rules in 47KB of CSS.
- Interactive borders raised from 2.28:1 to 3.58:1, clearing the 3:1 requirement.
- The animated gold gradient ran as low as 4.06:1 and dipped below the AA threshold on a
  loop. Now 5.91:1.
- The service filters claimed to be tabs without being tabs, so screen readers announced a
  broken widget. They are toggles now.
- The running total announces when it changes; skip link added; marquees pause on keyboard
  focus, not just hover.

**Bug fixed:** the rotating badge in the hero rendered 686px of text on a 452px path, so
about a third of the lettering overlapped itself.

**Design and speed:** warmer ground, wider tonal range, neutral shadows in place of gold
glows, tighter corners, a shorter preloader hold, and the hero now serves a WebP that was
already on the server but never referenced.

## What only Jordy can do

This is the real work. Nothing here is a website change.

### Google Business Profile — the single biggest gap

He has no profile surfacing, which means no presence in Maps.

**Before creating it, one question decides everything: is there permanent signage at
1407 Forest Drive naming his business specifically?**

- **Signage exists** → list as a hybrid: visible address plus a service area.
- **No signage** → list as a service-area business with the address hidden. This is the
  safe path and still ranks. Listing an address without signage is among the most common
  suspension triggers, and another barber operates from the same building, which raises
  scrutiny further.

Setup: primary category **Barber shop**, two or three honest secondary categories, the
business name exactly as it appears on signage with no keywords appended, and every service
filled in with a description and price. Services moved sharply up the ranking factors this
year and are the most commonly skipped field. Then connect Booksy as the booking link.

After verifying, wait about two weeks before editing anything. Rapid edits after
verification are themselves a suspension trigger.

### The review engine

He has 40 on Booksy and likely zero on Google. Booksy reviews do not transfer.

Ask every client, in the same words, every time. At the mirror, at the moment of the reveal.
Hand them a QR code and let them post later from their own phone. Two to three a week
sustained beats any burst, and consistency is the factor rather than raw count. Reply to
every review within a day, in the language it was written in.

Never offer anything in exchange, and never ask only the happy ones. Both are the cases
that trigger removal of everything.

### Booksy

- Rename the listing to match the site and Instagram. **Edit the existing profile — never
  create a new one**, because reviews attach to the profile and cannot be moved.
- Correct the ZIP to 21403.
- **Add the house call as a bookable mobile service.** The $200 product that the entire
  positioning rests on currently cannot be booked and appears nowhere on the listing.
- Reconsider the standing discount. It displays cuts at $27, which undercuts the premium
  story for anyone who checks.

### The name

The brand is fragmented across three surfaces: Instagram is `@jordycutzb`, Booksy says
"J.Barber 1", the site says "Jordy Cutz". Search engines can read inconsistent listings as
different businesses.

There is a sharper problem. A barber listed as "Jeison cutz" operates at the same address
with the same rating in the same category. "Jordy Cutz" sits one letter away from a direct
competitor in his own building.

The research argues for a surname-forward name for a $200 service, on the grounds that
high-end grooming names that way and that "Cutz" is the default construction of the $30
category. That is a real argument, but it is Jordy's call. **Consistency matters more than
which name wins.** One name, character for character, everywhere.

### Pricing

Studio services run $10–$50 and the house call is $200. There is nothing in between, so the
jump reads as arbitrary rather than as a different product. Research suggests a mid-tier
around $95 — a longer signature service — to bridge it, and a studio floor above the current
lowest price, which sits under the local market.

Full comparables and a recommended offer ladder are in `MARKET-RESEARCH.md`.

### Photography

The largest single lift available to the site's perceived quality, and it needs him.

The gallery photos are real, which is good, but they come from different phones under
different lighting, so they read as a camera roll rather than a body of work. One session,
one lighting setup, one crop ratio, ten to twelve images instead of thirty-two. Shoot the
client rather than the tools, crop tighter than feels comfortable, and skip anything with
a flash in a mirror.

A $200 brand cannot have $30 photography, and no amount of CSS fixes that.

The strongest feature angle: he works both ends of Forest Drive, the immigrant services

## Left to build

Roughly in order of value.

1. **Custom domain.** The current URL carries an unrelated account name, and on a GitHub
   Pages project path the robots file is never read, because crawlers only look at the
   domain root. About $11 a year fixes both. Do it before building citations, so listings
   do not need re-editing later.
2. **A phone number.** There is none anywhere on the site. Calls are a large share of local
   business actions and a number is required for consistent listings. Needs Jordy's number.
3. **Make the house call the hero product.** It is the premium offer and it is currently a
   toggle switch inside a sidebar. It deserves its own section.
4. **Separate pages for the mobile service and weddings.** A single page cannot rank for
   everything, and a dedicated page per service is the strongest local organic factor. Two
   real pages, not twenty templated city pages, which are treated as spam.
5. **Self-host the fonts.** Three families arrive as ten files through a render-blocking
   third-party stylesheet.
6. **Photography treatment.** A single grade applied across the gallery would unify mixed
   source material, but this is worth doing after a reshoot rather than before.
7. **Review dates on the cards.** Most people only weigh reviews from the last three months,
   and undated five-star quotes read as older than they are.

## Notes on the research

A large share of confident-sounding local SEO statistics circulate between marketing blogs
with no study underneath. Precise ranking-factor percentages, "review recency is the number
one factor", and per-suspension revenue figures could not be traced to primary sources and
are not repeated here. Where evidence was genuinely thin — most notably on whether purchased
clicks do anything — that is stated rather than resolved in either direction.

Two things worth verifying before acting: whether permanent signage exists at the studio,
which decides how the Google profile is created, and whether the intended domain and social
handles are actually available.
